# 0029: Recovering a dropped connection by ICE restart

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27
- Linear: FID-91

This record amends 0006, 0012, 0015 and 0019. Read them with the replacements under Decision.

## Context

- Once a data channel is open, the transfer runs without the server. When the route between the browsers drops, each page's connection goes to `failed`, and the page closes it with `connection-failed` (`onconnectionstatechange` in `apps/web/src/lib/peer.ts`). The sender's page refuses the recipient with `drop`. The recipient's page keeps the part that arrived, and its "Try again" continues from there on a new connection (SIG-92, "Continuing after a drop" in `docs/how-fidant-works.md`).
- A new connection is a new attempt with new certificates. On a link with the safety number, the two people compare a new number.
- 0019 added the sealed signals `restart-offer` and `restart-answer` for adding the relay. Each is accepted at most once per attempt, and a page ignores one that arrives while its ICE is connected or once its channel is open, and every later one. So nothing can restart ICE after the channel opened. 0019's Consequences name SIG-91 for recovering through an ICE restart.
- `joined` is the only message that carries ICE servers. Its TURN credentials are valid for 5 minutes (`apps/server/src/turn.ts`, 0006).
- The server answers a `join` from a socket that is already bound to a link with the error `already-joined` (`join()` in `apps/server/src/signaling.ts`). The recipient's page ignores an `error` once its channel is open (`apps/web/src/receive/ReceivePage.tsx`).
- The recipient's signaling socket stays open during a transfer. If it drops after the channel opened, the page closes its signaling client and opens no new socket. The sender's page ignores the server's `left` once the channel is open.
- While both signaling sockets are open, the server can already end a transfer in progress: a page closes with `bad-signal` when a sealed first `offer`, `answer` or `commit` of its attempt comes again (`handleSignal` in `peer.ts`), and the server can replay one, because sealed signals carry no counter (`apps/web/src/lib/keys.ts`).
- On 2026-11-27 the user chose the scope, who triggers the restart, and what the relay may do during recovery (see Decision).

## Decision

A transfer whose route drops after its data channel opened is recovered by an ICE restart on the same connection, while both pages' signaling sockets stay open. The recipient asks by sending its `join` again. The data channel, the two certificates and the safety number stay the same, and the transfer goes on where it was.

### Scope

- Recovery needs both signaling sockets. A recipient whose socket is gone can't ask, and a sender whose socket dropped has lost its binding on the server.
- A recipient that got `host-gone` in this attempt doesn't ask. The sender's socket dropped then, and joining its new socket would be the re-pairing that this record leaves out.
- Everything outside that scope ends as before this record: the page closes with `connection-failed` when its browser reports `failed`, and the recipient's "Try again" continues the file.
- Pairing again after a lost signaling socket, which a real change of IP address needs, is a follow-up issue, FID-349.

### The recipient asks

- After its data channel has opened, each time its `connectionState` becomes `disconnected`, the recipient's page sends the same `join` again on the same socket, at once: the same link, the same attempt ID and, on an emailed link, the same token. It sends no `gate` first.
- When the state becomes `failed`, it asks only while a deadline runs (see "The deadline"). A `failed` with no repeated `joined` in that drop closes the connection at once, and the page sends nothing.
- It asks once per such change of state and never on a timer, because each ask is one of the link's pairings.
- Before its channel has opened it never asks. A failed setup ends as before.
- It doesn't ask once the attempt has ended or the transfer has stopped, for example after RECEIPT or DECLINE: the sender has closed its connection then, and the same `join` would be answered with `used` or start a new connection.
- The sender's page never asks.

### The server answers a repeated `join`

- A `join` from the socket that is already bound to that link with that attempt ID is a repeated join. The server answers it like a first one: a new `joined` to the recipient and then to the sender, with ICE servers built as for any `joined`, so with new TURN credentials when the sender allows the relay now and a relay is up.
- On an email-bound link the repeated `join` must carry a token that the link's binding issued, as every `join` must (0012).
- A repeated join counts toward the link's 10 pairings per 30-second window (`pairingsPerLink`). Over the cap, the server answers `refused` with `busy` and keeps the recipient bound.
- On an email-bound link, a repeated `join` without such a token is answered like a first one, with `gate` and an empty address, and logged as `refused` with `unverified`. It keeps the recipient bound too.
- It logs the same `joined` line as a first join, and over the cap the same `refused` line with `busy`. There is no new log event.
- Any other `join` on a bound socket, for another link or with another attempt ID, still gets `already-joined`.

### The sender restarts ICE

- A `joined` for a recipient whose transfer is active, with that transfer's attempt ID, makes the sender's page restart ICE on that connection instead of refusing with `busy`. A `joined` with another attempt ID is refused with `busy` as before.
- The sender sets the new ICE servers (`setConfiguration`), calls `restartIce()` and sends a new offer as `restart-offer`.
- While that offer waits for its answer, a later repeated `joined` only sets its ICE servers and makes no second offer: a second offer would carry new ICE credentials, and the answer to the first would then be applied to it, leaving the two sides with credentials that don't match. The wait ends with the next `restart-answer`, applied or ignored, or when the connection is `connected` again. An offer or answer that is lost is not sent again; the deadline ends that recovery.
- Until its own data channel has opened, the sender's connection ignores a repeated `joined`: it restarts nothing and starts no deadline. The sender's page still takes the relay choice from it (see "The relay during recovery").
- If the sender's page has already ended the attempt, it holds no attempt ID to match, and the same `join` is a first join for it: refused with `used` after delivery, and otherwise the start of a new connection, which the recipient's page, having ended that attempt, doesn't answer.
- The recipient, from the first time its open connection reported `disconnected` in this attempt, applies every `restart-offer` that is in order and answers with `restart-answer`, whatever its ICE state and whether or not its channel is open. That is when its page asks, but the rule follows the state, not the `join`: it holds also when the page sent none, as after `host-gone`. Before that, 0019's rules hold unchanged.
- The sender, once it has got a repeated `joined` with its channel open, applies a `restart-answer` only while an offer of its own is waiting for an answer (`signalingState` is `have-local-offer`), and ignores any other. Before that, 0019's rules hold unchanged.
- A `restart-offer` or `restart-answer` that can't be sent during a recovery doesn't close the connection, as it does under 0019. The deadline ends it if no route comes back.
- Every restart SDP must still name the two certificates of the first `offer` and `answer` (0019, 0017). A page refuses another fingerprint as a bad signal.
- A second `offer`, `answer` or `commit` is still refused as a bad signal.

### The relay during recovery

- A page uses `turn:` and `turns:` URLs in a recovery only if this connection had already added the relay (0019). Otherwise it sets only the `stun:` URLs of the new list. So a pairing that connected directly still reserves no relay address, also when its route drops.
- A connection that had added the relay gets the new list as it is. When that list has no `turn:` or `turns:` URL, because the sender refuses the relay by then or no relay is up at that moment, the restart gathers no relay address, and a transfer that needs the relay is not recovered.
- A sender that refuses the relay passes only `stun:` URLs to its connection, as on the first `joined` (0008).
- The sender's page takes its relay choice again at each repeated `joined`: `turn` from the message and the box as it stands then. That choice replaces the one it stored for the attempt, so 0008's backstop judges a path read after a recovery by the choice at the latest `joined`.

### The deadline

- A page starts a deadline of 30 s (`RECOVERY_TIMEOUT_MS` in `peer.ts`) at a repeated `joined` that it acts on, unless one already runs. Its connection becoming `connected` ends the deadline.
- A deadline that starts while the page's own connection is `connected` and stays so runs its 30 s and then does nothing. A second drop within those 30 s gets only the time that is left.
- When the deadline runs out and the connection is not `connected`, the page closes with `connection-failed`. That is the same ending, with the same messages, as before this record.
- While a deadline runs, the browser's `failed` doesn't close the connection. Without a repeated `joined`, `failed` closes it at once, as before, so a page whose other side is gone waits no longer than today.

### The path

- Each time its connection becomes `connected` again after the channel opened, a page reads the path again and shows it. The sender then still ends an attempt that turns out relayed on a link that refuses the relay (0008).
- A page whose connection never left `connected` during a recovery reads nothing again. The sender's didn't in two of the six measured runs below, so the sender's page doesn't always read the path after a recovery.
- The sender doesn't report the path to the server again. The server's `path` line still describes how the connection first opened.

### What the recipient's page ignores

Once its data channel is open, the recipient's page lets none of these answers to a repeated `join` end the transfer:

- `refused` with `busy`: the server's cap, or a sender's page from before this record.
- Any `refused` after `host-gone` in this attempt: the server's answer to a `join` that crossed it.
- `gate`: the server's answer to a repeated `join` whose token it didn't take.
- `error`: a server from before this record answers `already-joined`.

`refused` with `drop` or `used` still ends the attempt. A `joined` for an attempt the page has ended is ignored, as before.

### What stays

- No new signaling message and no new sealed kind. `FRAME_VERSION` doesn't change.
- A page makes one `RTCPeerConnection` per attempt. A recovery makes no new certificate and no new safety number, so it is not a new attempt, and not a new guess in the sense of 0017.
- Flow control (SIG-50) and continuing after a drop (SIG-92) are unchanged. They run on the same channel.
- The pages show no new text. Progress stops during the drop and goes on after it.

Times are from the cut, on the host's clock, which adds 0.2 to 0.4 s.

- **Chromium kept the data channel through that outage and lost no frame.** The file arrived with a matching SHA-256, on one `RTCPeerConnection` per page, with a new local ICE password on both pages, and the sender never showed the transfer as interrupted.
- **The recipient's browser reported `disconnected` 5.3 to 7.5 s after the cut.** Its second `join` went out 3 to 7 ms later, and `joined` reached it 140 to 190 ms after that, once about 600 ms.
- **The sender's browser reported `disconnected` 4.3 to 6.5 s after the cut in four runs and never left `connected` in two.** It never reported `failed`, so the repeated `joined` reached the sender before its browser reported `failed`.
- **The restart's offer and answer completed on both pages about 200 ms after `joined`, while the route was still cut.** The sender's `restart-offer` went out 5 to 13 ms after its `joined`. Each run had exactly two `join` messages and one `restart-offer`.
- **The recipient was `connected` again 300 to 400 ms after the route came back.** Its state went from `disconnected` to `connected`, never through `connecting`.
- **Chromium also got through that cut without the restart.** With the sender's restart turned off, the route came back by itself after the restore and the file arrived; only the check for a new ICE password failed. So the test shows that the restart runs and does no harm on an outage this short, not that such an outage needs it.

- **When a browser reports `failed`.** Comments in `SendPage.tsx` and `ReceivePage.tsx` say that ICE consent runs out about 15 s after a page goes away. Nothing in this repository measures it.
- **A repeated `join` on `failed`** while a deadline runs, and **a second repeated `joined` before the first restart's answer**. Unit tests in `apps/web/src/lib/peer.test.ts` cover the rules (the second case in "a second rejoined before the answer makes no second offer, and the answer is applied"); no browser ran them.
- **An outage longer than about 8 s**, and **the 30 s deadline in a browser**. The deadline is a unit test with mock timers.
- **A recovery on a relayed connection, and whether a browser makes new TURN allocations in a restart.** If it does, a relayed transfer older than 5 minutes needs the new credentials, which the new `joined` brings. The experiment: cut and restore a relayed transfer that is more than 5 minutes old, with a temporary relay log (0007's runbook). Not run.
- **Which ICE generation's candidate pair carries the data after a recovery.**
- **Firefox and WebKit.** Nothing in this record was tried in either. The networks suite runs Chromium only.

## Consequences

- A transfer whose route drops and comes back within 30 s of the repeated `joined` goes on by itself, on the same connection, with the same safety number, without the recipient doing anything.
- A change of IP address that drops a page's WebSocket is not recovered. Neither is a drop in which the recipient's repeated `join` reaches the sender only after the sender's browser has reported `failed`: the sender's page has then closed the connection and refused the recipient with `drop`. Both end as before, and "Try again" continues the file.
- A direct route that doesn't come back is not replaced by the relay. That transfer fails 30 s after the repeated `joined`.
- The server learns more than before. The repeated `join` tells it that this transfer's connection dropped, and when the recipient's browser noticed. A second `joined` line for a link prefix, or a `refused` line with `busy` over the cap, followed by more `relay` lines, puts that in the log. Before this record, the server saw nothing of a pairing after the sender's `path` report, apart from the sockets closing and the sender's `refuse`. It doesn't learn why the connection dropped, on which side, how far the transfer had got, or whether the recovery worked. `docs/how-fidant-works.md` ("Every path and its traces") and the `/how/traces` text say so.
- What the server gains, on any link: it can send a `joined` nobody asked for and make the sender restart ICE. The recipient then ignores the `joined`, and the `restart-offer` too, unless its connection has reported a drop in this attempt. It can also replay a sealed `restart-offer` or `restart-answer` of this attempt once the pages apply more than one. The worst this does is end the transfer, which the server can already do by replaying a first `offer` or `answer` (see Context). It can't read or change the file: every restart SDP must name the two first certificates.
- What the holder of an emailed link's signal key gains (our server, 0012): once the recipient's connection has reported a drop, it can seal a `restart-offer` with candidates of its choice and so move the encrypted stream onto a route through an address it picks, mid-transfer. It could already pick the route at setup, by rewriting the candidates in the first offer. It sees what a relay sees: both IP addresses, sizes and times, and ciphertext under DTLS keys it doesn't have. The certificates stay pinned, so the safety number, where the link has one, still covers the connection.
- Each repeated join is one of the link's 10 pairings per 30-second window. A connection that flaps often enough is answered with `busy`, which the recipient's page ignores once its channel is open, and its next ask can succeed in the next window.
- A connection that had added the relay gets new credentials and may reserve relay addresses again in a recovery. Those count toward the relay's `total-quota` (0007) like the first ones. Whether a browser does reserve again is not measured.
- A sender who unticks the relay during a relayed transfer changes nothing until a recovery. The next repeated `joined` then carries no TURN URL, the restart gathers no relay address, and a path that still reads `relay` afterwards ends the attempt (0008's backstop).
- Each ICE restart makes the browsers gather candidates again, so they ask our STUN service for their public address again. That is how ICE defines a restart; it is not measured here.
- Version skew, with no change to `FRAME_VERSION`:
  - A recipient page from before this record never asks. Its transfers end as before.
  - A sender page from before it answers the repeated `joined` with `refuse` and `busy`. The server then unbinds the recipient and forwards `refused` with `busy`, which the new recipient page ignores once its channel is open. No restart happens, and the recipient's page closes when its deadline runs out, unless the route came back by itself.
  - A server from before it answers `already-joined`, which the recipient's page ignores once its channel is open. With no repeated `joined`, both pages end as before.
- `pnpm e2e` gains no test. The cut-and-restore test is in the networks suite (`pnpm e2e:networks`). A restart that never connects is a unit test with mock timers.
- Firefox and WebKit are untested, and Chromium is measured for one outage length on a direct path, as listed under "What this relies on outside our code".
